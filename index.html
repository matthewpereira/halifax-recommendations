Halifax Recommendations

From Matthew Pereira

Peggy's Cove

Iconic lighthouse, rocky Atlantic shoreline, fisherman's homes, gift shop, tourists. Remember to take a selfie with Clawsby the lobster.

The Waterfront

Expect crowds on weekends, or for it to be deserted in bad weather, but walking along the waterfront is great for people-watching, lets you see boats (from old/new military vessels to rich peoples' megayachts to dinghys), buskers, Georges Island, and has great food (from snacky food stall stuff to 5 star restaurants). The Maritime Museum is here, too.

The Shore Club

If you want the most authentic lobster dinner, and/or if you like live music, this is just a short drive from Halifax. After the last seating, patrons move the tables and chairs to the walls to convert it from a restaurant to a dance floor. It's been going for 80 years, and has bands playing basically every weekend. Most are local, but top Canadian bands and some international talent plays here regularly too.

Halifax Seaport Farmer's Market

At the very southern point on the waterfront are Halifax's seaports, and each weekend there's a farmer's market from pier 19 to pier 25. It's the longest running market in Canada (since 1750!), and while a lot of it is targeting local grocery shopping (and you don't necessarily want to buy a bunch of fresh produce or seafood when you're on vacation) there's lots of ready-to-eat, baked goods, good coffee, and other treats to snack on while you walk around.

Lunenburg, NS

If you're renting a car, this town about an hour from Halifax is a UNESCO world heritage site, with classic east coast painted cedar homes, some of the best seafood (we like The Fish Shack, but they're all good), ice cream parlors, vintage stores, bookstores, galleries, etc. It's also the home of the Bluenose II, a perfect replica of the famous racing schooner which is on Canada's 10 cent coin and NS license plates.

Art Gallery of Nova Scotia

It's not as good as the AGO in Toronto, full disclosure, but it has a nice little permanent collection including Maud Lewis' original home and several of her originals and a Kent Monkman, and usually has decent rotating exhibitions as well.

The Maritime Museum

If you're a museum/history type of person, this has an extensive collection to explore. It also has a Titanic exhibit and an exhibit for the Halifax Explosion in 1917, which was the largest man-made explosion before the atomic bomb. There are two real naval ships outside on the water you can go onboard and inside, along with all sorts of scale models and artifacts inside (along with the boats).

Dartmouth

This is Halifax's sister city on the opposite side of the bay. You can take the ferry from downtown Halifax (lower water street) to Dartmouth for something like $3, and it will come with a paper transfer so that you can ride back without paying anything extra as long as you return in 3 hours. It is SMALL: you can walk the main street (Portland Street) from the waterfront in about 15 minutes round trip. The ferry ride isn't exciting but is a memorable thing to do as a tourist: you can get pictures of Halifax's waterfront from the ocean, spot seals (and sometimes sharks, very rarely whales) in the water, take pictures of the bridges, feel the wind in your hair.

Go to Cafe Goodluck for a coffee and treat (some of the best breakfast sandwiches and baked goods are here), or go to Dear Friend bar for a cocktail, or get a sweet treat at Portland Street Creperie. Larry's has excellent sandwiches and iconic limited edition merch. Try a flight of ciders at Lake City. There's a shop that combines Morley's Coffee / Taz Records / Friction Books: good coffee, a small but decent selection of new and used records (at least a third of my used records are from here) and a truly great selection of poetry/philosophy/surrealist/politics/feminist/art/etc books (a good portion of my wife's favorite books of the past few years were found here).

Things I forgot

  1. People will tell you to go to Cow's Ice Cream on the waterfront (there are two locations now): it's famous as Canada's best ice cream, and you will enjoy it, but don't feel like you're missing out if you get the urge to skip it, there's always a big line and it's pretty commercial and touristy nowadays. I'd consider it optional.

  2. I didn't mention the Halifax Citadel national historic site: the fortress up on the hill in the center of the peninsula. I'd suggest you go for a walk here (if you're okay with climbing a hill!) to see the city from the top, and check out the clocktower, and it's a great place to sit with a snack (we will often grab something from Willy's) and watch the sunset or hang out. RE actually going into the museum, I wouldn't put it at the top of the list: it's mostly old cannons and old soldier uniforms and stuff like that, I didn't find it as cool as the Maritime museum personally.
